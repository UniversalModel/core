## A Three-Dimensional, Fifteen-Axis Rubric and Universal-Civilizational Leadership Scale

## 0. WHY THIS FRAMEWORK EXISTS

Conversations about "Theories of Everything" repeatedly collapse into two failure modes:

| Failure Mode | What Goes Wrong |
|--------------|-----------------|
| **The Crackpot Index trap** | Mixes scope (how much is claimed) with personality markers (grandiose tone, persecution narrative, conspiracy claims). Rewards modesty in voice rather than rigor in substance. |
| **The Single-Number trap** | Reduces incommensurable theories (a physical TOE vs. a governance TOE vs. a metaphysical TOE) to one ranking. Loses essential information. |

TEF avoids both by:

1. **Splitting "ambition" from "warrant" and "civilizational impact"** along separable axes.
2. **Using fifteen sub-criteria** with rubric-based 0-10 scoring.
3. **Plotting theories on a plane** (CA × EW) instead of a line.
4. **Defining regions** that name the resulting types honestly.
5. **Deriving a Universal-Civilizational Leadership Score** for cases where the question is not "which narrow model is most validated?" but "which candidate universal framework best combines breadth, warrant, and impact on living systems?"

The framework is *theory-agnostic*: it does not privilege physical, social, or metaphysical theories. It evaluates each on what it *claims* to be doing and how *well* it does it.

### 0.1 Non-negotiable core criteria

Before any leadership score is interpreted, TEF keeps four older, simpler criteria explicit:

| Core criterion | TEF implementation | Why it matters |
|----------------|--------------------|----------------|
| **Universality / Scope** | CA1 Domain Breadth + CA2 Scale Span + CA3 Structural Closure | A "universal" theory must actually cross domains, scales, and structural levels. |
| **Depth** | CA3 Structural Closure + EW2 Foundation Rigor + EW4 Scope Discipline | A theory must reach toward necessary primitives, not merely collect surface analogies. |
| **Coherence** | EW1 Internal Coherence + consistency across CA/EW/CI claims | A large theory that contradicts itself is not universal; it is fragmented. |
| **Real Grounding** | EW2 Foundation Rigor + EW5 Empirical Track Record + CI5 Deployability / Feedback | A theory must touch reality through mathematics, evidence, measurement, deployment, or falsifiable feedback. |

These are not extra applause points. They are **legitimacy gates**. A candidate can have a high-looking scalar score, but if it fails universality, depth, coherence, or real grounding, it cannot honestly be described as a leading universal theory.

## 1. THE THREE MASTER DIMENSIONS

### 1.1 Dimension I — CLAIM AMBITION (CA)

> *What is the theory asserting? How big is the bite it takes?*

CA measures the **scope and audacity of the assertions themselves**, independently of whether they are correct. A theory of the common cold has low CA; a theory unifying physics, biology, ethics, and AI alignment has high CA. CA is not a virtue or a vice — it is a description.

**Raw range:** 0 – 50 (sum of five 0-10 sub-axes).  
**Normalized range:** 0 – 100 (`CA_total × 2`).

### 1.2 Dimension II — EPISTEMIC WARRANT (EW)

> *How well-supported is what is asserted? What survives critical pressure?*

EW measures the **quality of the support** the theory marshals for whatever it claims. A modest claim with strong evidence and a grand claim with proportional evidence both score high on EW. EW is what scientific virtue actually rewards.

**Raw range:** 0 – 50 (sum of five 0-10 sub-axes).  
**Normalized range:** 0 – 100 (`EW_total × 2`).

### 1.3 Dimension III — CIVILIZATIONAL IMPACT (CI)

> *If the theory were used correctly, what scale of real-world improvement could it plausibly organize?*

CI measures the theory's **designed capacity to improve living systems**: health, well-being, longevity, planetary resilience, productive coordination, institutional service, and quality of life. CI is not moral applause and not historical popularity. It asks whether the theory contains operational pathways that could affect the conditions under which humans, institutions, ecosystems, and civilizations survive and flourish.

**Raw range:** 0 – 50 (sum of five 0-10 sub-axes).  
**Normalized range:** 0 – 100 (`CI_total × 2`).

### 1.4 The CA × EW Plane plus CI Depth

Every theory is one point on a 100×100 plane. Comparison is geometric, not linear.

```
              EW (Epistemic Warrant)
              ▲
          100 │
              │  Mature        Established
              │  Specialized   Universal
              │  ⚪            ★ (rare)
              │
           50 │  ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─
              │
              │  Trivial /     Speculative
              │  Inert         Universal
              │  ⚫            ⚠
            0 └────────────────────────────────►
              0           50            100
                       CA (Claim Ambition)
```

CI adds the third coordinate: `(CA, EW, CI)`. A theory can be broad and warranted but have little direct civilizational application; it can be useful but narrow; or it can be broad, warranted, and operationally relevant to life, welfare, resilience, productivity, and public service.

## 2. THE FIVE CLAIM AMBITION (CA) AXES

Each scored 0–10 with the rubric in §4.

### CA1 — Domain Breadth

How many *distinct epistemic domains* the theory integrates.

A "domain" is a field with its own native vocabulary, methods, and validation standards (e.g. quantum physics, thermodynamics, information theory, ethics, governance, AI alignment, cosmology, biology). Mere mention does not count; the theory must use the domain's structure.

### CA2 — Scale Span

How many *orders of magnitude* the theory spans, from sub-atomic to cosmic / civilizational. A theory limited to one scale (e.g., social only) scores low; one that explicitly claims to apply from quark to civilization scores high.

### CA3 — Structural Closure

Does the theory claim to provide a **minimal complete basis** (an irreducible set from which everything in scope can be reconstructed)? Examples of structural closure claims: "exactly three categories", "one universal formula", "a single operating system of existence". A descriptive list of useful concepts has low closure; a claim of necessity-and-sufficiency has high closure.

### CA4 — Predictive Reach

Does the theory generate **new outputs** (predictions, classifications, derivations) that did not exist before it? An assembly of existing ideas under a new label has low reach; a system that yields novel testable predictions or new diagnostic categories has high reach.

### CA5 — Operational Reach

Can the theory be **applied to act**, not just to describe? This includes governance protocols, measurement instruments, design rules, intervention sequences. A pure ontology has low operational reach; a theory that ships protocols, scoring formulas, and decision rules has high operational reach.

## 3. THE FIVE EPISTEMIC WARRANT (EW) AXES

Each scored 0–10 with the rubric in §4.

### EW1 — Internal Coherence

Are the parts logically consistent? Are there visible contradictions, equivocations, or hidden circularities? Does the same term mean the same thing in every section?

### EW2 — Foundation Rigor

How solidly does the theory's basis connect to established mathematics, physics, or formal logic? Distinguish three sub-modes:
- **Derivation** — formal proof from axioms (highest).
- **Isomorphism** — structural mapping with explicit bridge conditions (middle).
- **Analogy** — suggestive resemblance without bridge (lower, but non-zero).

A theory that claims derivation but supplies analogy scores *worse* than one that honestly claims analogy and supplies analogy. Honesty about mode is part of rigor.

### EW3 — Falsifiability

Is there a clearly specified observation, experiment, or logical test that would refute the theory? Are the falsifiability conditions stated explicitly and *attached to specific claims* rather than vague disclaimers? Theories that are unfalsifiable at every level score 0; theories with a published falsifiability matrix score high.

### EW4 — Scope Discipline

Does the theory **mark its own limits**? Does it distinguish what it proves from what it conjectures, what is derivation from what is analogy, what is established from what is speculative? The U-Theory L1/L2/L3 ladder is a model example. Absence of such marking — "everything I say is equally proven" — collapses this score to 0.

### EW5 — Empirical Track Record

What independent validations exist for the theory's claims? Score by:
- Number of independent replications.
- Diversity of domains validated.
- Whether validations were *risky* (could have falsified) or *cherry-picked* (chosen to confirm).
- Track record of corrections after disconfirmation.

A new theory with no validation track record scores low here even if other EW axes are strong — and that is correct. EW5 *should* lag for novel work; that is why it is one axis among five, not the only one.

## 3B. THE FIVE CIVILIZATIONAL IMPACT (CI) AXES

Each scored 0-10 with the rubric in §4. CI is deliberately separate from CA and EW: a theory may claim everything (high CA), prove much (high EW), and still do little for living systems (low CI).

### CI1 — Living-System Benefit

Does the theory provide concepts, metrics, or protocols that can improve health, well-being, longevity, quality of life, or reduction of suffering? This includes medical stability scoring, longevity hypotheses, mental/social well-being, and prevention of collapse in biological or social systems. Mere inspirational language scores low; operational health/welfare instruments score high.

### CI2 — Planetary and Existential Resilience

Does the theory address risks to long-duration survival at planetary, ecological, technological, AI, or civilizational scale? A theory that only optimizes a local subsystem scores low; one that models systemic fragility, cascading failure, AI governance, ecological stability, and existential-risk response scores high.

### CI3 — Human Flourishing and Civic Service

Does the theory improve the service relationship between institutions and human beings? This includes governance quality, citizen service, fairness, institutional responsiveness, education, dignity, rights, and public-value production. A theory that ignores lived human institutions scores low; one that offers civic protocols and measurable governance diagnostics scores high.

### CI4 — Productivity, Efficiency, and Resource Leverage

Does the theory increase output per unit cost without destroying stability? This includes decision efficiency, process optimization, energy/resource discipline, coordination efficiency, waste reduction, and better allocation under constraints. A theory that describes reality without improving action scores low; one that supplies optimization protocols and efficiency metrics scores high.

### CI5 — Deployability, Measurement, and Feedback

Can the theory be deployed as instruments, checklists, scores, software, protocols, or policy routines with feedback loops? This criterion prevents pure utopianism: impact must have a path from concept to measurement to correction. Real deployments and independent users raise the score; undocumented ideals cap it.

**Civilizational anti-gaming rule:** CI scores above 6 require an explicit mechanism of action; scores above 8 require either operational artifacts in the corpus (scoring tools, protocols, case studies) or independent analogues showing that the mechanism can affect real systems. A theory with harmful or destabilizing prescriptions can be capped at CI ≤ 3 even if it is ambitious.

## 4. SCORING RUBRIC (0 – 10 for every axis)

| Score | Meaning | Generic Calibration |
|------:|---------|---------------------|
| **0** | Absent or pathological | Criterion not engaged with at all, or actively violated. |
| **2** | Vestigial | Token gesture, not load-bearing. |
| **4** | Partial | Engaged unevenly; serious gaps. |
| **6** | Solid | Engaged competently; would survive non-expert review. |
| **8** | Strong | Engaged at expert level; survives most peer scrutiny. |
| **10** | Exemplary | Best-in-class; sets the standard for this criterion across all theories. |

**Anti-gaming rule:** A score of 8 or higher requires citable evidence within the theory's own corpus or independent literature. Self-assessment alone caps at 6.

**Tie-breaking rule:** Round down. If a criterion sits between 6 and 7, score 6.

## 5. AGGREGATION AND UNIVERSAL-FRAMEWORK LEADERSHIP SCALE

```
CA_total = CA1 + CA2 + CA3 + CA4 + CA5         (range 0–50)
EW_total = EW1 + EW2 + EW3 + EW4 + EW5         (range 0–50)
CI_total = CI1 + CI2 + CI3 + CI4 + CI5         (range 0–50)

CA_normalized = CA_total × 2                   (range 0–100)
EW_normalized = EW_total × 2                   (range 0–100)
CI_normalized = CI_total × 2                   (range 0–100)
```

The point `(CA_normalized, EW_normalized, CI_normalized)` is the theory's full TEF coordinate.

### 5.1 TEF coordinate

The coordinate `(CA_normalized, EW_normalized, CI_normalized)` is the primary output. It should always be reported before any scalar summary.

### 5.2 Universal-Framework Leadership Score (UFLS)

For one specific question — **"which candidate universal framework best combines scope and warrant?"** — TEF defines a scalar leadership score:

```
UFLS = CA_normalized × EW_normalized / 100      (range 0–100)
```

This is the **product**, not the sum — so a theory must score on *both* dimensions to score high. A theory with CA=100 and EW=10 scores 10. A theory with CA=10 and EW=100 also scores 10. A theory with CA=70 and EW=70 scores 49. This rewards genuine universality + warrant simultaneously, and punishes claim-without-warrant or warrant-without-reach.

UFLS is not a truth score. It is a leadership score for universal-framework candidates. A narrow model with extraordinary empirical warrant may dominate EW while still losing UFLS because it does not attempt universal integration. That is not a defect; it is exactly what the scale is designed to measure.

### 5.3 Universal-Civilizational Leadership Score (UCLS)

When the comparison question explicitly includes **impact on living systems and planetary/civic welfare**, TEF uses:

```
UCLS = CA_normalized × EW_normalized × CI_normalized / 10000      (range 0–100)
```

UCLS is stricter than UFLS. A theory cannot lead merely by being grand, merely by being well-validated, or merely by being socially useful. It must combine:

1. **large scope** (CA),
2. **defensible support** (EW),
3. **operational civilizational relevance** (CI).

This is the correct scalar for ranking theories by potential impact on living life, well-being, longevity, planetary durability, happiness/flourishing, productivity, efficiency, and service to citizens.

### 5.4 Core Legitimacy Gate (CLG)

UCLS should be interpreted only after the theory passes the Core Legitimacy Gate:

```
Universality_Core = (CA1 + CA2 + CA3) / 30 × 100
Depth_Core        = (CA3 + EW2 + EW4) / 30 × 100
Coherence_Core    = EW1 × 10
Grounding_Core    = (EW2 + EW5 + CI5) / 30 × 100
```

Recommended thresholds for a **universal leadership claim**:

| Gate | Pass threshold | Interpretation |
|------|---------------:|----------------|
| Universality_Core | ≥ 70 | The theory is genuinely cross-domain / cross-scale / structurally ambitious. |
| Depth_Core | ≥ 70 | The theory reaches toward deep primitives and marks the level at which its foundation is axiomatic, isomorphic, empirical, or conjectural. |
| Coherence_Core | ≥ 60 | The theory is internally intelligible and non-contradictory. |
| Grounding_Core | ≥ 50 | The theory has some real basis: formal structure, empirical contact, or deployable feedback. |

If a candidate fails any gate, TEF may still score it descriptively, but it cannot be called a leading universal-civilizational framework. This prevents a theory from winning by only one dimension: pure grandness, surface breadth, pure evidence in a narrow domain, or pure humanitarian rhetoric.

**Depth note (Newton rule).** TEF does not require every successful theory to reduce all its primitives to a deeper ontology. Newtonian mechanics did not prove what gravity "is" in ultimate depth; it introduced stable primitives and laws that organized observations with extraordinary power. Likewise, a universal framework may receive depth credit when it identifies minimal primitives, states their role clearly, connects them to physical/mathematical reality, and uses them productively without pretending to derive everything from zero.

### 5.5 Strict analytic comparison rule

Given a fixed candidate set `T` and agreed TEF axis scores, a theory `A` is the **unique UCLS leader** iff:

```
UCLS(A) > UCLS(B) for every B in T, B ≠ A
```

The proof is arithmetic, not rhetorical. The only disputable step is the axis scoring; once the axis scores are accepted, the leadership result follows mechanically.

**Robustness test:** For any rival `R`, theory `A` remains ahead iff:

```
CI_A > (CA_R × EW_R × CI_R) / (CA_A × EW_A)
EW_A > (CA_R × EW_R × CI_R) / (CA_A × CI_A)
CA_A > (CA_R × EW_R × CI_R) / (EW_A × CI_A)
```

This gives an explicit sensitivity margin instead of a vague claim of superiority.

## 6. THE FIVE REGIONS

TEF uses regions, not a one-dimensional ranking. The default boundary between low and high ambition is CA = 50; the default boundary between weak and substantial warrant is EW = 50. For "established universal" status, the warrant requirement is stricter, because universal claims require proportional support.

### 6.1 Q1 — Established Universal

Operational definition: **CA ≥ 70 and EW ≥ 75**.

The theory takes a large bite *and* delivers warrant proportional to it. Historical examples are exceedingly rare: General Relativity after the 1919 eclipse within its physical domain, and possibly the Standard Model inside particle physics after multiple precision tests. Most universal candidates aspire to this region; almost none arrive.

### 6.2 Q2 — Live Universal Candidate (CA ≥ 50, 40 ≤ EW < 75, or CA ≥ 50 with substantial but non-established warrant)

High ambition with partial-to-substantial warrant, but not yet established-universal warrant. Internally coherent, with explicit scope discipline and at least some falsifiability and validation. This is where serious cross-domain frameworks live during their working lives. **U-Theory v26 sits here** (see §7.1) and so does the Wolfram Physics Project.

### 6.3 Q3 — Speculative Universal (CA ≥ 50, EW < 40)

High ambition without warrant. The theory claims a great deal but supplies little defensible support. This is the historic home of "lone-genius TOEs". The work may still contain useful ideas, but the warrant gap means it cannot yet act as a working framework.

### 6.4 Q4 — Mature Specialized (CA < 50, EW ≥ 50)

Modest ambition with high warrant. Most working science lives here: a well-validated model of one phenomenon. Not "small" — just narrow. ESG frameworks, Gallup Q12, ISO 26000, and most peer-reviewed empirical work occupy this quadrant.

### 6.5 Q5 — Trivial / Inert (CA < 50, EW < 50)

Modest ambition without warrant. Lists, frameworks, and rebrandings without theoretical bite or empirical depth. Not pathological — just unremarkable.

### 6.6 The Pathological Off-Plane: "Crank"

A theory belongs off the regular plane as "crank-pathological" when its warrant collapses structurally, regardless of whether its ambition is high or merely noisy:

- EW < 10
- Refuses to mark its own limits (EW4 = 0)
- Treats every disagreement as conspiracy
- Cannot specify what would refute it (EW3 = 0)

Belongs *off* the regular plane in a separate notation. The point of separating CA from EW is that **claim ambition alone is not pathology** — the pathology is claim ambition combined with epistemic abdication. This is a structural diagnosis, not a personality judgment.

## 9. HOW TO USE TEF

### 9.1 For self-assessment

1. Score your own theory honestly using §4. Self-scores cap at 6 unless externally citable.
2. Identify the binding constraints — the 2 lowest sub-axis scores.
3. Choose one constraint per quarter to improve. Do not chase all five.
4. Re-score quarterly. Track the trajectory, not the snapshot.

### 9.2 For comparison

1. Score every theory using the *same evidence standard*.
2. Plot the full `(CA, EW, CI)` coordinate.
3. Compare *within regions*; cross-region comparison is structurally misleading unless the explicit UFLS or UCLS leadership question is being asked.
4. Report the score *with* the justification per axis. A score without justification is opinion.

### 9.3 For peer review

1. Reviewer scores independently of author.
2. Disagreement on any axis ≥ 3 points triggers axis-level discussion.
3. Final score is reviewer's, not author's.
4. Inter-rater reliability across ≥ 3 reviewers is the benchmark for stable scoring.

## 10. WHAT TEF DOES *NOT* DO

To be honest about its own scope (per its own EW4 criterion):

- TEF does **not** decide whether a theory is *true*. It measures structural ambition, warrant, and civilizational impact capacity, not truth.
- TEF does **not** replace peer review, replication, or experimental test. It organizes them.
- TEF does **not** decide truth with a single number. UFLS and UCLS are leadership diagnostics for specified candidate sets and questions, not rankings across reality.
- TEF does **not** punish ambition. A high CA with proportional EW is the highest-scoring profile possible.
- TEF does **not** reward modesty as a virtue. A low CA is just low CA.

## 12. OPEN ISSUES (HONESTLY)

- **Subjectivity of rubric calibration.** Two careful raters can differ by 1–2 points per axis; aggregated, this is ±10–20 on the 100-point CA, EW, or CI. TEF needs an inter-rater reliability study before claiming hard precision.
- **CA4 / EW5 coupling.** A theory that has not yet been published or tested cannot score high on EW5 even if EW1–EW4 are strong. This is correct in principle but penalizes new work; users should track *trajectory* not just snapshot.
- **Domain weighting.** Currently every domain in CA1 counts equally. A version 2 might weight by structural distance — integrating physics + ethics is a bigger bite than integrating sociology + political science.
- **No formal proof of axis orthogonality.** TEF *claims* the fifteen axes are independent. Empirically they may correlate (e.g., EW4 and EW1, or CA5 and CI5, may move together). Future work: factor-analyze a corpus of scored theories.
- **Potential vs realized impact.** CI measures designed civilizational impact capacity, not total historical realized impact. Mature technologies may deserve a separate RI (Realized Impact) appendix.

- **Specialized theories are not demoted.** Quantum Mechanics, Evolution, Maxwell electromagnetism, Thermodynamics, Information Theory and Theory of Computation legitimately top the *raw* UCLS table. TEF marks them as Mature Specialized rather than Universal Candidate; they continue to dominate their own domains and to feed UCLS into any future universal framework.
- **Civilizational impact for U-Theory is partly prospective.** CI = 86 includes potential impact through deployment of U-Score, GSI-RTD, Discovery Protocols and SSS. If those deployments do not materialize, CI5 should drop and UCLS will fall accordingly. The framework is explicitly designed to lose its leadership status if its deployment promise is not honored.
- **Future revisions.** New theories may enter and existing scores may rise. The framework is intended to be auditable, not final.

### 14.7 What it would take to dethrone U-Theory

TEF v1.3 leadership is fragile, on purpose. Any of the following would push U-Theory off the gate-passing #1 spot:

1. Another Universal Candidate raises EW above 64 while keeping CA ≥ 80 and CI ≥ 70.
2. U-Theory's deployment fails (CI5 falls below 5), pulling UCLS below 30.
3. A new framework explicitly closes the universality gate (CA1, CA2, CA3 each ≥ 8) AND grounds at least three sub-claims in independent empirical/mathematical derivations (EW2 ≥ 8, EW5 ≥ 7).

This is what "strict and defensible" means in TEF: the leader can be replaced by a clearly better framework on published, recomputable axes.
